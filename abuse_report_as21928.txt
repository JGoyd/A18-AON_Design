------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY
------------------------------------------------------------------------
A reproducible architecture analysis (linked below) shows that the
host `pancake.apple.com` -- the endpoint from which iOS devices fetch
the plist-encoded "bag" that configures telemetry filtering for the
`rtcreportingd` daemon and several other Apple processes -- is being
served from a CDN node deployed inside T-Mobile USA address space,
i.e. inside AS21928.

The placement is reproducible from any iOS device on a stock OS, using
only Apple-provided diagnostic tooling. No third-party instrumentation
is required.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY THIS IS BEING REPORTED TO T-MOBILE
------------------------------------------------------------------------
The pancake.apple.com bag is the configuration surface that decides
which fields are stripped from iOS telemetry before it is uploaded.
In the captured responses:

  - Identifying fields such as `_clientName`, `_clientVersion`, and
    `_sender` are removed by the bag rules.
  - Content metadata (hostnames, URIs, byte counts, sync state) is
    preserved and forwarded.
  - The bag responses themselves carry no content-level cryptographic
    signature -- their integrity is bound only to TLS to the serving
    node.

A serving node for that bag living in carrier address space rather
than in Apple-registered space is, at minimum, an undocumented
deployment topology. As the operator of AS21928, T-Mobile is the only
party who can confirm:

  1. Whether the IP(s) serving pancake.apple.com inside AS21928 are
     in fact assigned to Apple under a hosting / interconnect /
     on-net-cache arrangement, or are otherwise authorized to serve
     that hostname from your network.
  2. Whether that arrangement is publicly documented anywhere, and
     if so, where.
  3. Which T-Mobile contact is appropriate for follow-up technical
     questions about the deployment.
